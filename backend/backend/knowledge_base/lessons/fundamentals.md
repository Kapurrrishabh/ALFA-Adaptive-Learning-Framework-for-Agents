# Fundamental analysis

## P/E ratio (price to earnings)
Share price divided by earnings per share. Trailing P/E uses the last reported
12 months or fiscal year; forward P/E uses analysts' estimates. A high P/E means
investors pay more per rupee of current earnings, usually because they expect
growth. P/E is only comparable within similar businesses: banks, IT services and
FMCG companies trade in very different ranges. It is not meaningful when earnings
are negative. Whether a P/E is "expensive" depends on growth, return on capital,
risk and interest rates, which is why this system shows it next to growth and
ROE rather than alone.

## P/B ratio (price to book)
Market capitalization divided by shareholders' equity (book value). Most useful
for banks, insurers and asset-heavy businesses whose book value is close to
economic value; less useful for asset-light companies.

## EV/EBITDA and EV/Sales
Enterprise value (market cap + debt − cash) divided by EBITDA or revenue. Because
EV includes debt, these ratios compare companies with different capital
structures more fairly than P/E.

## PEG ratio
P/E divided by the expected earnings growth rate (in percent). A PEG near 1 is a
rough rule of thumb for growth priced "fairly"; it depends heavily on the growth
estimate used.

## Free cash flow and FCF yield
Free cash flow is operating cash flow minus capital expenditure: the cash the
business generates after reinvestment. FCF yield is free cash flow divided by
market capitalization. Persistent profits without free cash flow deserve scrutiny.

## ROE, ROA and ROIC
Return on equity = net income / shareholders' equity. Return on assets = net
income / total assets. Return on invested capital = after-tax operating profit
(EBIT × (1 − tax rate)) / invested capital. ROIC above the cost of capital is the
core sign of a business that creates value; high ROE can be inflated by leverage,
so it should be read with debt/equity.

## Margins
Gross margin = gross profit / revenue; operating margin = operating income /
revenue; net margin = net income / revenue. The trend matters as much as the
level: expanding margins signal pricing power or operating leverage.

## Debt/equity, interest coverage and current ratio
Debt/equity = total debt / shareholders' equity. Interest coverage = EBIT /
interest expense; below about 2 means earnings barely cover interest. Current
ratio = current assets / current liabilities, a measure of short-term liquidity.
These ratios are not meaningful for banks and NBFCs, whose business is borrowing
and lending; capital adequacy and asset quality (gross and net NPA) matter there.

## Growth
Revenue, net income, EPS and free-cash-flow growth are measured year on year and
as a multi-year CAGR. Growth measured from a loss (negative base) is not
meaningful and is not reported as a percentage.

## Indian reporting conventions
Indian listed companies report under Ind AS (converged with IFRS). Under SEBI's
Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements, quarterly results are due within
45 days of quarter end, and audited annual results within 60 days of the fiscal
year end (fiscal years usually end on 31 March). Companies publish both
standalone and consolidated figures; consolidated figures include subsidiaries
and are generally the right basis for valuation.
