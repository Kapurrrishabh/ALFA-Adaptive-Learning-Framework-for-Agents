# Fundamental analysis

## P/E ratio (price to earnings)
The share price divided by earnings (profit) per share. Trailing P/E uses the
last reported 12 months or fiscal year. Forward P/E uses analysts' estimates. A
high P/E means investors pay more for each rupee of current earnings, usually
because they expect growth. P/E is only comparable within similar businesses.
Banks, IT services and FMCG companies trade in very different ranges. P/E is not
meaningful when earnings are negative. Whether a P/E is "expensive" depends on
growth, return on capital, risk and interest rates. That is why this system
shows it next to growth and ROE, not alone.

## P/B ratio (price to book)
Market capitalization divided by shareholders' equity (book value). It is most
useful for banks, insurers and asset-heavy firms, whose book value is near
economic value, and less so for asset-light firms.

## EV/EBITDA and EV/Sales
Enterprise value (market cap + debt − cash) divided by EBITDA (a profit figure)
or revenue. EV counts debt, so these ratios compare firms with different capital
structures more fairly than P/E.

## PEG ratio
P/E divided by the expected earnings growth rate (in percent). A PEG near 1 is a
rough rule of thumb for growth priced "fairly". But it depends heavily on the
growth estimate used.

## Free cash flow and FCF yield
Free cash flow is operating cash flow minus capital expenditure: the cash a
business generates after reinvesting. FCF yield is free cash flow divided by
market capitalization. Be wary of steady profits with no free cash flow.

## ROE, ROA and ROIC
Return on equity = net income / shareholders' equity. Return on assets = net
income / total assets. Return on invested capital = after-tax operating profit
(EBIT × (1 − tax rate)) / invested capital. ROIC above the cost of capital is the
core sign that a business creates value. High ROE can be pumped up by leverage
(borrowed money). So read it with debt/equity.

## Margins
Gross margin = gross profit / revenue. Operating margin = operating income /
revenue. Net margin = net income / revenue. The trend matters as much as the
level. Rising margins can mean pricing power or operating leverage.

## Debt/equity, interest coverage and current ratio
Debt/equity = total debt / shareholders' equity. Interest coverage = EBIT /
interest expense. Below about 2, earnings barely cover interest. Current ratio =
current assets / current liabilities. It measures short-term liquidity: whether
near-term obligations can be met. These mean little for banks and NBFCs, whose
business is borrowing and lending. What matters there is capital adequacy and
asset quality (gross and net NPA: bad debts).

## Growth
Revenue, net income, EPS and free-cash-flow growth are measured year on year,
and as a multi-year CAGR (yearly rate, compounded). Growth from a loss (a
negative base) is not meaningful. It is not reported as a percentage.

## Indian reporting conventions
Indian listed companies report under Ind AS, which is close to global IFRS
rules. Under SEBI's Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements, quarterly
results are due within 45 days of quarter end. Audited annual results are due
within 60 days of the fiscal year end, most often 31 March. Companies publish
standalone results (the firm alone) and consolidated results (with its
subsidiaries). The consolidated ones are generally the right basis for
valuation.
