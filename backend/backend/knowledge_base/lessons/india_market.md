# Indian equity market essentials

## Exchanges and indices
India's two main stock exchanges are the National Stock Exchange (NSE) and BSE
(formerly Bombay Stock Exchange). The NIFTY 50 is NSE's benchmark index of 50
large companies; the S&P BSE Sensex tracks 30. Bank Nifty tracks the most liquid
(most traded) banking stocks. Sector indices (Nifty IT, Nifty Pharma, Nifty FMCG
and others) track industries. The markets regulator is SEBI.

## Trading hours and settlement
Regular equity trading runs from 9:15 am to 3:30 pm IST, Monday to Friday. There
is a pre-open session from 9:00 am. Equity trades settle (are completed) T+1,
one working day after the trade. Exchanges publish a holiday calendar each year.
Always check the current year's list.

## Price bands and circuit breakers
Individual stocks can have daily price bands (for example 2%, 5%, 10% or 20%).
They cannot trade beyond the band that day. Many large, derivative-eligible
stocks have no fixed band, only dynamic limits. Market-wide circuit breakers
halt trading if the NIFTY 50 or Sensex moves 10%, 15% or 20% in a day.
Surveillance frameworks (ASM and GSM) can impose extra restrictions on specific
stocks.

## Transaction costs
Costs include brokerage and GST on brokerage. Then there are Securities
Transaction Tax (STT), exchange transaction charges, SEBI fees and stamp duty.
STT on delivery equity trades (shares you hold) is 0.1% on both buy and sell.
Round-trip delivery trades through a discount broker often cost about 0.1–0.15%
of trade value. Intraday (same-day) and frequent trading cost more as a share of
returns. Verify current rates with your broker.

## Taxes on listed equity (verify current rules)
Long-term capital gains (profit on shares held over 12 months) are taxed at
12.5% on gains above ₹1.25 lakh per financial year. Short-term gains are taxed
at 20%. These hold from 23 July 2024 for listed equity with STT (securities
transaction tax) paid. Dividends are taxed at your income tax slab rate. Tax
rules change with Union Budgets, so confirm them for the relevant year before
acting.

## Corporate disclosures
Under SEBI's LODR rules, listed companies must disclose their results and major
(material) events to the exchanges. These filings on NSE and BSE are first-hand
(primary) sources. They are more reliable than press coverage. Quarterly results
are due within 45 days of quarter end.
